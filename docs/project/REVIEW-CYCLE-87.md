# 🔄 Review & Remediation: Cycle 87

## 🎯 Objective
Protect participants' **Flow State** during the "Independent Sprint" by minimizing interruptions from the instructor and other participants.

## 📋 Findings & Remediation

| Area | Finding | Severity | 2-Axis Impact | Remediation |
|------|---------|----------|---------------|-------------|
| **Flow State** | The Independent Sprint (00:45-01:15, 30 min) is the longest individual work segment and the core of the learning experience. However, `INSTRUCTOR-QUICK-REF.md` does not define an "Interruption Protocol" for this phase. In practice, instructors frequently interrupt participants to answer questions, provide hints, or correct direction — even well-intentioned interruptions break the cognitive flow state. Cognitive science shows it takes an average of 23 minutes to fully regain focus after an interruption (Mark et al., UC Irvine). The workshop's 30-minute Sprint window means even 2-3 interruptions can destroy the flow window. No "suggestion box" or "parking lot for Sprint questions" exists. | **High** | **All Paths:** The Independent Sprint is where participants apply their Jungle Title skills independently. This is the highest-value segment for learning outcomes. Flow state disruption during this phase directly reduces skill acquisition and participant confidence. | Added **Section 8 — Flow State Protection** to `INSTRUCTOR-QUICK-REF.md` with: (1) A "No Interruption" rule during the Sprint (instructors circulate silently, note questions on a whiteboard, address in batch), (2) A "Flow Pass" system — participants who need help get a visible token so the instructor can approach without verbal interruption, (3) A "Question Parking Lot" on the instructor's whiteboard where questions are written and addressed after the Sprint. |

## 📐 Risk Assessment
- **Risk Score:** 4 × 4 = **16** → **Critical**
- **Probability:** Very Likely (4) — instructor interruption behavior is a near-universal pattern in workshop settings without explicit protocols.
- **Impact:** Affects ALL paths. Flow state disruption during the Independent Sprint (the core learning segment) degrades the primary learning outcome. Participants lose the deep-focus experience that distinguishes this workshop from passive lecture formats.

## 🔄 R&R Rule Derivation
This cycle produced **Rule PW-3** in Section 19 of `R&R-CYCLE-RULES.md`:
> **Rule PW-3 — Flow State Protection:** During the Independent Sprint (and any other dedicated work phase), instructors must NOT verbally interrupt participants. Instead: (1) Circulate silently and observe progress, (2) Write participant questions on a "Question Parking Lot" whiteboard and address them as a batch at the phase transition, (3) Provide "Flow Pass" tokens — participants who need help receive a physical/digital token signaling the instructor to approach non-verbally. This protects the cognitive flow state that is essential for skill acquisition during deep work.
